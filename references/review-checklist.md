# Complete the editorial review

Use this checklist for a full editorial review and the writer's whole-draft self-review. For a narrow request, apply the relevant checks within its scope. It supplements the detailed rules; it does not replace them with a shorter list. Surgical editing means the smallest **effective** repair. Adding an article or substituting a verb is insufficient if the reader still cannot follow the thought.

## Review each passage, then the whole scope

For each in-scope paragraph or equivalent unit, check the following. Read the surrounding paragraphs to judge connections. A short message can receive this review directly. For a substantial file review, keep a compact internal coverage note by paragraph or section, with findings or a concrete keep reason for each applicable dimension. Link repeated findings to their actual locations. Do not create a second research ledger or deliver this internal record unsolicited.

| Check | Test before marking it complete |
|---|---|
| **REVIEW.1 Reader knowledge** | Can the intended reader follow the people, documents, events, terms, pronouns, and option labels from this text? Explain an unfamiliar document type or a change of label. Earlier chat context is not reader knowledge. Preserve useful articles and relation words under P1. |
| **REVIEW.2 Substance** | Does an opening state an actual point, action, event, or relation? Does each abstract label receive enough explanation? Ask “which people?”, “what action?”, “what information?”, and “how does this relate?” where the wording leaves those questions unresolved. “Adds context,” “offers perspectives,” and “receives separate treatment” need the actual operation when it is unclear. Necessary technical terms stay; an agent-created label is not automatically adopted terminology. |
| **REVIEW.3 Connected development** | Can the reader explain why each sentence follows the previous one? A date or fact needs its supported role in the comparison or explanation. A connector cannot supply a missing relationship. Apply FLOW within and across paragraphs; expand a compressed relation when the reader needs more words. |
| **REVIEW.4 Voice and action** | Check grammatical voice separately from vague or impersonal prose. “The argument would grow” is active, but its metaphor may conceal what the author proposes. “We would review” is active; “the file would be reviewed” is passive. A modal does not determine voice. Keep the active-voice house rule, and also require an intelligible subject, action, and object where relevant. Never invent an actor. |
| **REVIEW.5 Qualification necessity** | Apply Q1 **before polishing a caveat**. Which specific misleading inference or consequential condition requires it here? Delete an obvious or irrelevant warning when none does. If needed, explain the actual limit and its consequence at the relevant point; “limits the assessment” may leave both unclear. Preserve substantive uncertainty and conditions. Do not append an assurance that the prose avoids overstatement or guarantees no result. A request to avoid unsupported findings calls for accurate wording, not a closing disclaimer announcing that care. |
| **REVIEW.6 Modal purpose** | Apply the modal review below to each use and to clusters across the scope. A necessary modal can stay; its grammatical legitimacy does not settle whether repeated framing obstructs the passage. |
| **REVIEW.7 Recurring form** | Check empty openers, aphoristic summaries, generic verbs, clause skeletons, repeated modal frames, and formatting under M1–M3. Review the sequence even when every sentence is individually grammatical. Retain useful parallelism and repeated terms. Recheck repairs for a new stock substitute. |
| **REVIEW.8 Correctness and fidelity** | Apply every relevant grammar, form, usage, spelling, lexical-ban, punctuation, and source-attribution rule. Does the proposed wording preserve facts, evidence status, logical conditions, citations, and protected choices? Do source summaries explain the relevant argument accurately, beyond naming a topic? Missing source detail requires a flag or authorized source reading, never an invented expansion. |

Before proposing or delivering, read **all in-scope prose**, including unchanged passages, as a reader. Compare each repair with its baseline and name the obstacle it actually resolves. A zero-match scan, a rule label, or “checked flow” cannot substitute for that reading. Mark a dimension as satisfactory, addressed by a proposed/internal repair, outside scope, protected, or unresolved with a reason. Do not present unresolved passages as fully repaired. No fixed number of edits or numerical style threshold defines success.

For existing text, this check finds proposals; it does not authorize applying them. During application, verify the approved changes and report any newly noticed issue separately. Applying all approved items proves that application is complete; it does not prove that the earlier audit found every problem. The writer repairs its initial draft internally and delivers finished prose without an unsolicited checklist transcript.

## Review modals without rotating synonyms

Review **would, may, might, could, can, should**, and other modal constructions in context. They are not individually banned. Separate a proposed action, a conditional consequence, a possibility, a capability, permission, and an obligation. Ask what changes if the modal disappears. Do not turn an untested explanation into a finding, a proposed plan into an approved undertaking, a capability into a completed act, or a condition into an unconditional prediction.

Repeated planning language often needs a structural repair. An established proposal frame can govern several related actions, allowing a connected sentence or paragraph without repeating **would** before every verb. State the actual prediction or uncertainty where it belongs. Do not replace every **would** with **will**, **may**, **I propose**, or a present-tense assertion. Vary structure according to the thought, not to meet a word-frequency target. Counts locate clusters; they neither condemn nor excuse them.

**Plan example.** Assume a review of three offices is proposed, interview outcomes are unknown, and comparison concerns their handling of requests.

Before: “The review would cover three offices. Interviews would explain the delays. The report would compare their procedures.”

After: “I propose a review of three offices, using interviews to learn why requests take so long and comparing how each office handles them.”

The proposal frame covers the actions. The revision states the intended learning without promising that interviews will produce an answer. It is one possible repair, not a paragraph template.

**Necessary condition.** “If the supplier misses Friday's deadline, the delay would postpone Monday's installation.” Keep the condition and consequence if that is the supported plan. Do not remove the modal merely because another paragraph overuses it.

## Test the actual reading obstacle

- **Empty framing:** Assume the delivery record supplies promised dates. “The delivery record illustrates the problem. Four orders arrived late.” can become “Four orders arrived after the delivery dates promised to customers.” The substance replaces the announcement. Do not add those dates or promises without support.
- **Unsolicited caveat:** “Missing invoices would limit the review” can be deleted when invoice completeness has no bearing on the requested decision. If the task requires a complete expense total, “We cannot calculate the full expense total until the missing invoices arrive” explains a relevant consequence. The task determines necessity; a longer caveat is not automatically a better one.
- **Opaque source summary:** “The report links staffing and delays” still hides its argument. If the supplied report says that overnight requests wait because no staff cover that shift, state that mechanism accurately. Without that source content, flag the missing explanation. A fluent invented account is a failed repair.

Use these tests across genres. They are editorial decisions, not evidence about authorship, and they do not create new lexical bans.
